\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:6}

In this paper we have described basic principles of operation for
iterative hash functions in general and MD5 in particular. We then
described the naming schemes used within Centera and mapped the
properties described earlier to the Centera Content Address (CA). We
demonstrated the likelihood of a CA collision in a Centera cluster.
Finally, we concluded that hash functions will continue to evolve, and
that the Centera system is architected to quickly adjust its naming
scheme strategy to the latest insight in the relative strengths of
cryptographic hash algorithms.

The corrected probability calculations require explicit randomness and workload
assumptions. Small accidental-collision probabilities do not establish
adversarial collision or second-preimage resistance. GM write-path behavior
and a concrete M++ second-preimage analysis remain unresolved.
